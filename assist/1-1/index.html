    1.1 Bob in your flow - Tab Completion & Next Edit Prediction

    Module 1 — ASSIST · Bob in Your Flow

    This is the first stage of the curve, and the gentlest. Here, Bob works shoulder to shoulder with the developer inside the editor — anticipating the next keystroke, drafting the next few lines, making targeted edits on request — while the developer accepts or rejects every single change. The value to pitch here is immediate and low-risk: your teams move faster without breaking their train of thought, and nothing lands in the codebase that they did not explicitly choose. It is the most natural possible on-ramp to working with an AI partner, and it pays off from the very first session.

    Why this matters

    The quickest way to lose a developer's confidence in an AI tool is to pull them out of their flow. Bob's first two capabilities are designed to do the opposite — to keep a developer in the work while quietly removing friction from it. They are tab completion and next edit prediction, and they operate as a pair: the first suggests code from context, the second anticipates where you will need to go next.

    How tab completion works

    Tab completion is a familiar idea executed unusually well. Where conventional autocomplete reacts to the few characters you have just typed, Bob reads the surrounding context to make a genuinely informed suggestion — drawing on the comments describing your intent, the patterns and structure already present in the code, your project's conventions and style, and related code in other files. The result feels less like word-completion and more like a colleague finishing your thought.

    Accepting suggestions

    When Bob offers a suggestion, you stay in charge of it:

    • Press Tab to accept it
    • Keep typing to override it
    • Watch it update dynamically as you continue

    Next edit prediction

    Real code changes are rarely confined to one spot. Add a field, and you often need to use it elsewhere; introduce a function, and something downstream has to call it. Next edit prediction is built around that reality: after you accept a suggestion, Bob anticipates the related edit you are likely to need and moves you there. The rhythm is simple — describe what you want in a comment, let Bob suggest the code, press Tab to accept, and press Tab again to jump to the next predicted location, where Bob positions your cursor exactly where the new code needs to be used.

    Expect some variation. Generative AI is non-deterministic, so your results may differ from what you see here. If an output diverges, refine the prompt, follow up with another, or adjust the code by hand — or simply carry on if what you have works.

    Hands-on: adding email validation with Next Edit Prediction

    A reminder before you start: because you are working with generative AI, your results may not match the screenshots exactly — and that is perfectly fine. Better still, most hiccups can be resolved without touching a line of code yourself. If you notice a discrepancy between this guide and what you see on screen, describe it to Bob in plain language; more often than not, it will sort the problem out for you.

    1. Navigate under the UserIdentification function (this should be around line 19-21 in UserIdentification.tsx), and type:

    1. With //validate email addresses in place, press Enter, then Tab.
